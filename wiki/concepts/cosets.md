# Cosets

## Formal Map Formulation

For any $a \in G$, define the **left and right operation maps**:

$$L_a : G \to G,\quad L_a(g) = a \star g$$
$$R_a : G \to G,\quad R_a(g) = g \star a$$

Both $L_a$ and $R_a$ are **bijections** on $G$.

The **left coset** $aH$ and **right coset** $Ha$ are the images of $H$ under these maps:

$$aH = L_a(H) = \{a \star h \mid h \in H\}$$
$$Ha = R_a(H) = \{h \star a \mid h \in H\}$$

---

## Definition

Let $G$ be a group and $H \le G$ a subgroup. For any $x \in G$:

$$\text{Left coset:}\quad xH = \{xh : h \in H\}$$

$$\text{Right coset:}\quad Hx = \{hx : h \in H\}$$

- $x$ is the **coset representative**; $H$ ranges over the entire subgroup.
- Every coset is a subset of $G$ (since $H \subseteq G$ and $G$ is closed).
- When $G$ is abelian, left and right cosets coincide.
- Notation: $G/H$ (or $G \backslash H$ for right cosets) denotes the **set of all cosets** of $H$ in $G$.
- **Index** $[G:H]$ is the number of distinct left (or right) cosets of $H$ in $G$.

In additive notation (e.g., $(\mathbb{Z}_n, +)$): $a + H = \{a + h : h \in H\}$.

---

## Worked Examples

### Finite: $\mathbb{Z}_4$ with subgroup $H = \{0, 2\}$

$G = \mathbb{Z}_4 = \{0,1,2,3\}$, $H = \{0,2\}$.

Right cosets $H + x$:

| Representative $x$ | Coset $H + x$ |
|--------------------|--------------|
| $0$ | $\{0+0,\ 2+0\} = \{0, 2\} = H$ |
| $1$ | $\{0+1,\ 2+1\} = \{1, 3\}$ |
| $2$ | $\{0+2,\ 2+2\} = \{2, 0\} = \{0, 2\}$ — same as $H$ |
| $3$ | $\{0+3,\ 2+3\} = \{3, 1\} = \{1, 3\}$ — same as coset for $1$ |

Only **two distinct cosets**: $\{0,2\}$ and $\{1,3\}$. They partition $\mathbb{Z}_4$.

### Infinite: $(\mathbb{R}, +)$ with subgroup $\mathbb{Z}$

The coset $\mathbb{Z} + 0.5 = \{\ldots, -1.5,\ -0.5,\ 0.5,\ 1.5,\ 2.5,\ \ldots\}$ — integers shifted by $0.5$.

Using a different representative from the same coset, e.g. $1.5$:

$$\mathbb{Z} + 1.5 = \{\ldots, -0.5,\ 0.5,\ 1.5,\ 2.5,\ \ldots\} = \mathbb{Z} + 0.5$$

Same coset — confirming the key theorem below.

---

## Seven Properties of Cosets

Let $G$ be a group, $H \leq G$, and $a, b \in G$:

1. $a \in aH$.
2. $|aH| = |bH| = |H|$ (all cosets have equal size).
3. $aH = bH$ or $aH \cap bH = \emptyset$ (cosets are equal or disjoint).
4. $aH = bH \Leftrightarrow a \in bH \Leftrightarrow a^{-1}b \in H$.
5. $aH = Ha \Leftrightarrow H = aHa^{-1}$ (left = right coset iff $H$ is normal at $a$).
6. $(ab)H = a(bH)$ and $H(ab) = (Ha)b$.
7. The left (or right) cosets of $H$ in $G$ form a **partition** of $G$.

---

## Key Theorem: Equal or Disjoint

**Theorem**: If $Ha$ and $Hb$ are two right cosets of $H$ in $G$, then either $Ha = Hb$ or $Ha \cap Hb = \emptyset$.

Equivalently: if $a \in Hb$, then $Ha = Hb$.

**Proof sketch** (right cosets):

Suppose $a \in Hb$, so $a = h_1 b$ for some $h_1 \in H$.

- **(i) $Ha \subseteq Hb$**: Take any $x \in Ha$, so $x = h_2 a = h_2 h_1 b$. Since $h_2 h_1 \in H$ (subgroup closure), $x \in Hb$. $\checkmark$
- **(ii) $Hb \subseteq Ha$**: From $a = h_1 b$ we get $b = h_1^{-1} a$ (subgroup has inverses). Take any $y \in Hb$, so $y = h_3 b = h_3 h_1^{-1} a$. Since $h_3 h_1^{-1} \in H$, $y \in Ha$. $\checkmark$

Therefore $Ha = Hb$. $\blacksquare$

---

## Cosets Partition the Group

The distinct cosets of $H$ in $G$ form a **partition** of $G$:
- Every element $g \in G$ belongs to at least one coset (namely $Hg$, since $e \in H$ implies $g = eg \in Hg$).
- By the theorem above, any two cosets are either equal or disjoint.

So the distinct cosets are pairwise disjoint and their union is all of $G$.

---

## All Cosets Have Equal Size

For any $a \in G$, the map $h \mapsto ha$ is a bijection $H \to Ha$. Therefore $|Ha| = |H|$ for every coset. In particular all cosets have the same cardinality.

---

## The Identity Coset is the Only Subgroup

Among all cosets of $H$ in $G$, only $He = H$ (the coset generated by the identity) is itself a subgroup. Any other coset $Ha$ ($a \notin H$) does not contain $e$, so it cannot be a subgroup.

---

## Lagrange's Theorem

Since the cosets partition $G$ and each has size $|H|$:

$$|G| = [G:H] \cdot |H|$$

where $[G:H]$ is the **index** of $H$ in $G$ — the number of distinct cosets.

**Corollaries**:
- $|H|$ divides $|G|$ for any subgroup $H \le G$.
- The order of any element $g \in G$ divides $|G|$.
- Any group of prime order $p$ is cyclic and has no proper nontrivial subgroups (isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_p$).

---

## Normal Subgroups and Quotient Groups

When $H \trianglelefteq G$ (normal: $gH = Hg$ for all $g$), the cosets themselves form a group under $(aH)(bH) = (ab)H$ — the **quotient group** $G/H$.

Not every subgroup is normal: in $S_3$, the subgroup $\langle (1\ 2) \rangle$ has different left and right cosets.
