# Dihedral Groups

## Definition

A **symmetry** of a regular $n$-gon ($n \geq 3$) is a rigid motion that maps the polygon to itself. The set of all symmetries under composition forms the **dihedral group** $D_n$, with $|D_n| = 2n$.

The elements of $D_n$ are generated by:
- $r$ — counter-clockwise rotation by $\frac{2\pi}{n}$ radians
- $s$ — reflection about the line through the first vertex and the origin

$$D_n = \{1, r, r^2, \ldots, r^{n-1},\ s, sr, sr^2, \ldots, sr^{n-1}\}$$

The rotations $\{1, r, r^2, \ldots, r^{n-1}\}$ form a cyclic normal subgroup of order $n$ inside $D_n$.

## Example: $D_4$ (Square)

Label vertices: $1$ = upper-left, $2$ = lower-left, $3$ = lower-right, $4$ = upper-right.

| Symmetry | $1234 \to$ | Description |
|----------|-----------|-------------|
| $R^0$ | $1234$ | Identity |
| $R^1$ | $4123$ | 90° CCW rotation |
| $R^2$ | $3412$ | 180° rotation |
| $R^3$ | $2341$ | 270° CCW rotation |
| $V$ | $4321$ | Reflection (vertical axis) |
| $H$ | $2143$ | Reflection (horizontal axis) |
| $D$ | $1432$ | Reflection (diagonal, upper-left) |
| $D'$ | $3214$ | Reflection (diagonal, upper-right) |

### Cayley Table ($f \circ g$ means: apply $g$ first, then $f$)

| $\circ$ | $R^0$ | $R^1$ | $R^2$ | $R^3$ | $V$ | $H$ | $D$ | $D'$ |
|---------|-------|-------|-------|-------|-----|-----|-----|------|
| $R^0$ | $R^0$ | $R^1$ | $R^2$ | $R^3$ | $V$ | $H$ | $D$ | $D'$ |
| $R^1$ | $R^1$ | $R^2$ | $R^3$ | $R^0$ | $D$ | $D'$ | $H$ | $V$ |
| $R^2$ | $R^2$ | $R^3$ | $R^0$ | $R^1$ | $H$ | $V$ | $D'$ | $D$ |
| $R^3$ | $R^3$ | $R^0$ | $R^1$ | $R^2$ | $D'$ | $D$ | $V$ | $H$ |
| $V$ | $V$ | $D'$ | $H$ | $D$ | $R^0$ | $R^2$ | $R^3$ | $R^1$ |
| $H$ | $H$ | $D$ | $V$ | $D'$ | $R^2$ | $R^0$ | $R^1$ | $R^3$ |
| $D$ | $D$ | $V$ | $D'$ | $H$ | $R^1$ | $R^3$ | $R^0$ | $R^2$ |
| $D'$ | $D'$ | $H$ | $D$ | $V$ | $R^3$ | $R^1$ | $R^2$ | $R^0$ |

**Observations:**
- $R^0$ is the identity.
- $(R^1)^{-1} = R^3$; reflections are self-inverse ($D^{-1} = D$, etc.).
- $D_4$ is **non-abelian**: $R^1 \circ V = D \neq D' = V \circ R^1$.
- Each element appears exactly once in each row and column (Latin square property).
- Associativity is inherited from function composition.
