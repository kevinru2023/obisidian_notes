# Symmetric Groups

## Definition

Let $X$ be any nonempty set. The **symmetric group on $X$** is

$$S_X = \{\text{bijections } X \to X\}$$

with the operation of function composition. When $X = \{1, 2, \ldots, n\}$ this is written $S_n$ and called the **symmetric group of degree $n$**.

$$|S_n| = n!$$

The elements of $S_n$ are called **permutations**.

---

## Relationship to Permutation Groups

$S_X$ (or $S_n$) is a specific, maximal object — the group of **all** bijections on $X$.

A **permutation group** is any *subgroup* of some symmetric group. Every permutation group sits inside some $S_n$, but need not contain all of its elements.

> **Cayley's Theorem:** Every group $G$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of $S_{|G|}$. Symmetric groups are therefore universal — every abstract group can be realized concretely as a group of permutations.

---

## Cycle Notation

A **$k$-cycle** $(a_1\ a_2\ \cdots\ a_k)$ denotes the permutation

$$a_1 \to a_2 \to \cdots \to a_k \to a_1, \quad k \to k \text{ for all other } k.$$

**Composition is applied right to left.** For example, in $S_6$, the permutation $(245)(46)$ means: apply $(46)$ first, then $(245)$:

$$1\to1,\quad 2\to4,\quad 3\to3,\quad 4\to6,\quad 5\to2,\quad 6\to5.$$

A **transposition** is a 2-cycle $(i\ j)$.

---

## Seven Key Facts (Handout 3)

1. $|S_n| = n!$

2. Every element of $S_n$ factors uniquely (up to ordering) into **disjoint cycles**. By convention, start each cycle with its smallest element.

3. **Disjoint cycles commute.**

4. Every element of $S_n$ can be written as a product of transpositions (not uniquely). Example:
$$
(1234) = (14)(13)(12).
$$

5. The number of transpositions in any decomposition of a given $\sigma$ is always **even or always odd** — parity is invariant.

6. The **alternating group** $A_n$ is the subgroup of even permutations:
$$
A_n = \{\sigma \in S_n \mid \sigma \text{ is a product of an even number of transpositions}\}.
$$

7. $|A_n| = \dfrac{n!}{2}$.

---

## The Element Order Formula

The **order** of a permutation equals the LCM of its cycle lengths:

$$\text{ord}(\sigma) = \text{lcm}(\ell_1, \ell_2, \ldots, \ell_r)$$

where $\ell_1, \ldots, \ell_r$ are the lengths of the disjoint cycles in $\sigma$'s factorization.

---

## $S_3$ — The Canonical Small Example

$S_3$ is the **smallest non-abelian group** ($|S_3| = 6 = 3!$).

| Element | Cycle | Order | Parity |
|---------|-------|-------|--------|
| $\varepsilon$ | $()$ | 1 | even |
| $\alpha$ | $(2\ 3)$ | 2 | odd |
| $\beta$ | $(1\ 3\ 2)$ | 3 | even |
| $\gamma$ | $(1\ 2)$ | 2 | odd |
| $\delta$ | $(1\ 2\ 3)$ | 3 | even |
| $\kappa$ | $(1\ 3)$ | 2 | odd |

The three even elements $\{\varepsilon, \beta, \delta\}$ form $A_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}_3$.

**Sample composition** (right to left): $\beta \circ \gamma$ — apply $\gamma = (1\ 2)$ first, then $\beta = (1\ 3\ 2)$:

$$1 \xrightarrow{\gamma} 2 \xrightarrow{\beta} 1, \quad 2 \xrightarrow{\gamma} 1 \xrightarrow{\beta} 3, \quad 3 \xrightarrow{\gamma} 3 \xrightarrow{\beta} 2 \implies \beta \circ \gamma = \alpha = (2\ 3).$$

## Small Cases

| $n$ | $S_n$ | $|S_n|$ | Abelian? |
|-----|-------|---------|----------|
| 1 | $\{e\}$ | 1 | yes |
| 2 | $\mathbb{Z}_2$ | 2 | yes |
| 3 | smallest non-abelian | 6 | no |
| $n \ge 3$ | non-abelian | $n!$ | no |

---

## The Alternating Group $A_n$

$A_n \trianglelefteq S_n$ (normal subgroup, index 2).

- $A_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}_3$
- $A_4$ has order 12; contains a Klein-4 normal subgroup
- **$A_n$ is simple for $n \ge 5$** — no proper nontrivial normal subgroups. This is the key fact behind the insolubility of the general quintic (Galois theory).
