## Lecture 8 — Adversarial Search

**Agenda:** alpha-beta pruning; games with uncertainty.

### Key ideas
- **Game setting:** deterministic, discrete, finite, fully observable, two alternating players, zero-sum, time-limited.
- **Minimax:** $\text{Minimax}(n)$ is MAX's utility under optimal play by both sides. It maximizes MAX's worst case. Cost: $O(b^m)$.
- **Alpha-beta pruning:** track $\alpha$ (MAX's best so far) and $\beta$ (MIN's best so far), and prune when $\alpha \ge \beta$. It gives the same answer as minimax. With perfect move ordering the cost is $O(b^{m/2})$, about twice the lookahead depth.
- **Chance nodes:** expectimax and expectiminimax take probability-weighted averages at chance nodes. Equal probabilities are assumed unless the problem says otherwise.
- **Scale dependence:** with chance nodes, rescaling the leaf values can change the decision even when their order is preserved.
- **Modern use:** AlphaStar (self-play RL + best response + learned evaluation); adversarial ML as $\min_\theta \max_\delta$.
- **Limitations:** requires turn-taking (no simultaneous moves) and a strictly competitive game (MIN has no payoffs of its own).

### Formulas

$$
\text{Minimax}(n) =
\begin{cases}
\text{Utility}(n) & n \text{ terminal} \\
\max_{s} \text{Minimax}(s) & n \in \text{MAX} \\
\min_{s} \text{Minimax}(s) & n \in \text{MIN}
\end{cases}
\qquad
\text{Chance}(c) = \sum_{s} P(s)\,\text{value}(s)
$$
